## About me

My name is Marianna, and I am a bioengineer with a PhD in Computer Science, awarded by the [University of Genoa](https://unige.it/en) in July 2026. I completed my doctoral research at the [Perception and Interaction Lab](https://pilab.unige.it), within the [DIBRIS Department](https://dibris.unige.it/en).

You can read my PhD thesis, *[Designing Interactive XR Environments: Safety, Engagement, and Task Effectiveness Across Immersive and Non-Immersive Systems](https://unige.iris.cineca.it/handle/11567/1310177)*.

With a background in bioengineering and a passion for immersive technologies, I focus on the design, development, and evaluation of eXtended Reality (XR) applications. My research spans human–computer interaction, immersive visualization, and interactive technologies, with a particular interest in their applications to motor rehabilitation and education.

During my PhD, I also spent four months in Trondheim, Norway, working on the [XR4HUMAN project](https://xr4human.eu/) at the [IMTEL Lab](https://www.ntnu.edu/imtel/) of the [Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU)](https://www.ntnu.edu/). During this experience, I conducted UX and accessibility evaluations of XR applications with end users and investigated the accessibility of XR-based career guidance applications for people with cognitive impairments.

## My PhD work

I hold a Master's degree in Bioengineering (*110/110 cum laude*) and a PhD in Computer Science, specializing in eXtended Reality (XR) and Human–Computer Interaction.

During my PhD at the Perception and Interaction Lab of the University of Genoa, I worked on technologies for motor rehabilitation, particularly a suite of **wearable-controlled exergames for upper-limb rehabilitation in children**, developed within the [Fit4MedRob project](https://www.fit4medrob.it/).

My work involved:

- Software development and hardware–software integration.
- Design and execution of experimental studies.
- Data analysis and interpretation.
- Project documentation and research management.
- Collaboration with clinicians and research centers.

The system is intended for clinical testing with patients through a clinical trial approved by the Italian Ministry of Health. This research has also resulted in several scientific publications.

## Research interests

My main research interests include:

- Design, development, and evaluation of eXtended Reality (XR) applications.
- Immersive visualization technologies.
- Human–Computer Interaction (HCI) and user-centered design.
- Applications of XR in motor rehabilitation and education.

## Community involvement

Beyond my research activities, I have actively contributed to the XR research community.

I organized two editions of [XRehab](https://sites.google.com/view/xrehab/home), a workshop on XR for rehabilitation held within IEEE ISMAR: the [2025 edition](https://sites.google.com/view/xrehab/home) and the [2026 edition](https://sites.google.com/view/xrehab2026/home).

Throughout my PhD, I was also a member of the [DoCS group](https://docs-dibris.github.io), an association of Computer Science PhD students that promotes seminars and initiatives fostering collaboration and the exchange of ideas among doctoral candidates.

Within DoCS, I was the main organizer of the [Computer Science Workshop 2023](https://docs-dibris.github.io/docs/Computer%20Science%20Workshop/csw23.html) and the [Computer Science Workshop 2024](https://docs-dibris.github.io/docs/Computer%20Science%20Workshop/csw24.html).

The CSW is an event entirely organized by PhD students, designed to offer peers from different areas of computer science a broader perspective on the discipline. Each edition features academic speakers from diverse research fields, both in Italy and internationally, as well as contributions from local companies highlighting industry perspectives and research opportunities.

For three years, I also served as a PhD student representative within the DIBRIS Department, strengthening my organizational, communication, and coordination skills.
